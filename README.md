Most tech videos die in the first ten seconds. You spend forty hours benchmarking a GPU, tweaking lighting, and editing crisp B-roll—only for viewers to click away before you even finish your intro.

If you want to kill your drop-off rate, you need to master the **Open Loop Hook**.

Borrowed from Hollywood screenwriters, an open loop is a psychological technique that plants a mystery in the viewer’s brain. The human mind naturally craves closure. When you open a loop, the brain *refuses* to tune out until that loop is closed.

Here’s how most tech creators start a video:
*"Today we’re testing if the new M3 Max MacBook is worth $3,000."* (Boring. Instant swipe.)

Here’s that exact video using an Open Loop:
*"I spent $3,000 on this MacBook, but twelve hours before my review went live, I found a hidden flaw that completely ruined my render times. More on that in a second—first, let’s look at the benchmarks."*

See the difference? You’ve opened a loop. The viewer is now locked in because they need to know what broke. 

Another example for dev or software channels:
*"I let AI write my entire SaaS codebase, but five minutes before launch, a single line of generated code exposed every user’s API key. Here’s the exact bug that almost destroyed the project..."*

**The 3 Rules of Executing Open Loops:**
1. **Show the stakes early:** Feature the broken gear, the crazy benchmark anomaly, or the crashed server in seconds 0 to 5.
2. **Delay the resolution:** Deliver your core value while holding back the final answer until the climax.
3. **Always close the loop:** Never clickbait. Give them a satisfying payoff to build long-term viewer trust.

Stop starting videos with *"Hey guys, welcome back."* Open a loop, hold the tension, and watch your audience retention skyrocket.
