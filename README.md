Most tech videos die in the first 30 seconds. Why? Because dumping a list of benchmarks like a glorified spec sheet kills viewer retention fast. If you want people to stop scrolling and actually finish your videos, you need a battle-tested copywriting framework: **AIDA**.

Here’s how to use it to write tech scripts that hook, hold, and convert.

**1. Attention (The First 5 Seconds)**
Stop starting with "Hey guys, welcome back." Shock them, challenge a common belief, or show an insane visual hook. 
*Tech Example:* "This $50 open-source gadget just made my $2,000 smart home setup completely useless."

**2. Interest (The Payload)**
Now that you have them, build curiosity. Connect the cool specs to a relatable pain point.
*Tech Example:* "I put its new neural processor through a stress test with heavy 4K rendering, and the thermal results defied physics."

**3. Desire (The Emotional Flex)**
Make them crave the solution or the experience. Shift from *what* the tech is to *how it transforms their workflow*.
*Tech Example:* "Imagine exporting a full video project in seconds without your laptop fans sounding like a jet engine taking off."

**4. Action (The Next Step)**
Don't let the momentum fade. Give a single, crystal-clear directive.
*Tech Example:* "Drop a comment with your current setup, and hit subscribe for the real-world battery test."

AIDA turns dry tech reviews into binge-worthy stories by driving viewers through a logical emotional progression. Stop guessing your script structure—map your next video to Attention, Interest, Desire, and Action, and watch your retention graphs climb.
