Retention on tech videos is brutal. Viewers click for a quick fix, get their answer in fifteen seconds, and bounce. If you want to keep them glued past the two-minute mark, you need to master **The Curiosity Loop**.

It’s a psychological copywriting framework designed to trigger the brain's innate desire for closure. You open an information gap, withhold the final payoff while delivering steady value, and close the loop only after building maximum anticipation.

Here’s how to build one in your next tech script:

**1. Open the Loop (The Tease)**
Plant an unresolved question in the first 15 seconds. 
*Example:* "This $50 mechanical keyboard out-types my $300 custom setup—but only because of one bizarre software tweak."

**2. Escalate the Value (The Delay)**
Deliver on your video's core promise—review the build quality, switches, and sound profile—while holding back that specific tweak. Every subsection should answer a minor question while keeping the main mystery alive. You’re giving them immediate value, but keeping the ultimate answer locked.

**3. Close (and Chain) the Loop**
Finally give them the payoff. "The tweak? Remapping the actuation points using an open-source driver." But don't let the tension die—immediately open a *new* loop to push them to your next video: "Which brings up a massive security flaw most people ignore..."

**Why it works for tech creators:**
Tech reviews and tutorials easily devolve into dry spec-sheet readings. The Curiosity Loop turns a static product breakdown into a dynamic narrative. You aren't just listing features; you’re solving a mystery.

Structure your next script around two or three micro-loops. Keep the information gaps opening and closing, and watch your average view duration skyrocket.
