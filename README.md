Most tech videos die in the first 30 seconds. Why? Because your opening tells the viewer exactly what to expect.

If your hook is *"Today we’re reviewing the new M3 MacBook to see if it’s fast,"* you’ve already lost. Everyone knows it’s fast. There’s no mystery, no friction, and zero reason to keep watching.

Fix this with the **Logic Gap** framework.

The Logic Gap exploits cognitive dissonance. You present a universally accepted tech rule (Premise A), pair it with a completely contradictory real-world outcome (Result B), and hold the explanation hostage. 

Human psychology hates an open loop. The viewer *must* stay to bridge the gap.

Here is what it looks like in practice:

* **Standard Hook:** "The RTX 4090 is a beast, but it demands a lot of power."
* **Logic Gap Hook:** "The RTX 4090 is the fastest GPU on Earth, but pairing it with this $150 CPU actually *increased* my frame rates over a flagship i9. Here’s the bottleneck hiding in your system."

Notice the gap? A budget CPU shouldn't beat a flagship. It defies standard hardware logic, forcing the viewer to stick around to resolve the confusion.

Another example for workflow or coding channels:

* **Standard Hook:** "Here is why I switched from macOS to Linux."
* **Logic Gap Hook:** "Linux is supposed to give you total control over your hardware, but running it on my main rig completely locked me out of my own local AI models. Here’s the single driver flaw that broke everything."

To build a Logic Gap hook:
1. **Identify the standard tech assumption** (e.g., More RAM = faster render times).
2. **Present the rule-breaking result** (e.g., Adding 64GB of RAM doubled my render times).
3. **Delay the solution** past the 60-second mark.

Stop giving away the ending in your opening line. Create the gap, delay the bridge, and watch your retention curve flatten out.
